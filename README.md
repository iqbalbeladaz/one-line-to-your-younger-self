## The entry

`main.py` writes exactly one line to `/output/entry.txt` — the single line
the writer's older self would send back to his younger self:

> Don't sell the week they list — the coins you panic-sold in March paid for someone else's laptop, not yours.

108 characters, one line, one sentence, deterministic: the program writes
the same bytes on every run.

## Why this line

- **Actually told to a younger self:** an imperative warning, not a slogan
  addressed to nobody — "Don't sell the week they list".
- **Only this older self could say it:** drawn from a life actually lived —
  panic-selling coins the week they listed, in March, while farming airdrops.
- **One breath, real voice:** a single sentence, no explaining, no second
  sentence doing the work.
- **A turn that stays:** the coins didn't just vanish — they "paid for
  someone else's laptop, not yours", the sting of watching your own gain
  fund a stranger's win.
- **Honest enough to sting:** hard-won, not sentimental — the regret is
  specific, dated, and unfixable.

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py   # writes /output/entry.txt
```
